Why Cholistani desi ghee tastes different

Cows in Cholistan graze on desert grasses and shrubs and drink from tobas, the rainwater ponds of the desert. Their milk is rich, and ghee made from it slowly over a wood fire has a deep, nutty smell you will not find in factory tins.

We buy directly from herding families and small makers, pay them fairly, and pack everything ourselves in Bahawalpur.

1. Fresh milk is set into dahiWhole milk is boiled and set overnight in clay pots.
2. Churned into makhanThe dahi is hand-churned at dawn to lift the white butter.
3. Slow-cooked into gheeMakhan is cooked on a low fire until it turns golden and grainy (danedar).
4. Packed and sentSealed jars go out by courier. You pay when it arrives.
